walk in with a tiny white dog; a burst of clipper and comb close-ups later the man sees a groomed, muscular
gentleman in the mirror, and the dog gets the same glow-up for the last gag.

## Signature device
The makeover with a mirror reveal, run by a mascot craftsman. Three acts: an exaggerated "before" that enters
the shop (hair taken to absurd, monster-like levels so the change is unmistakable), a 3-4 s montage of 0.5 s
macro shots of tools meeting material (clipper teeth into curls, comb, blow-dryer) cut against the client's
worried reaction close-ups, then the reveal staged through the shop's own prop: we see the man from behind and
his new self in the mirror, hold 1.5 s, and wipe through the mirror into a front shot of him admiring himself
with the mascot proudly behind. A second, smaller payoff (the dog) lands the ending as a gag.

## Why it works
- Transformation is the most reliable retention structure: the before is so extreme that viewers must see the
  after.
- The mascot gives the business a face and an attitude (smug, proud); the client does the reacting.
- Macro tool shots make the craft tactile and satisfying.
- Double payoff: when the man's reveal is done, the dog's glow-up gives a second laugh and a rewatch.
- The caption carries the brand voice and the call to action ("if you want an ad people remember, DM me"),
  so the film itself stays clean.

## Build it in our studio
- Engine: `gen-video` (paid, approval) from our own styleframes for the 3D cartoon look; `cab` for a painterly
  2D version; `canvas` for a flat 2D version with a drawn mascot.
- Scene plan: 1) design the client's mascot and the "before" customer as one character sheet each; 2) five
  shots: entrance, appraisal, tool macro burst, reveal in the prop, second gag; 3) keep macro tool shots
  short (0.5 s) and cut on a beat; 4) build the reveal with a back view plus reflection and a wipe through the
  prop; 5) end on the mascot's proud pose or the second gag, then the logo for half a second if the client
  wants it.
- Hard part: keeping two characters consistent across generated shots and getting the "after" to read as the
  same person. Lock the face shape and outfit in the sheet; change only the hair.
- Difficulty 3; most of the cost is paid generation, not render time.
