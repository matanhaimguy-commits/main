## Signature device
Lyric literalism with a single hero object: one sung word at a time, small hand-lettered caps (about 45 px cap
height on a 1080 frame) centred around 27% from the top, and under it one centred illustration that acts out the
current phrase. The illustration never sits still: it carries a micro-cycle locked to the word grid (moon phase
steps every word, about 0.5 s; a small moon orbits the heart's tilted ring once every 1.5-2 s; leaves scale up on
stressed syllables and back down on the next word). Scene changes happen on phrase boundaries only (about 3.5, 10,
13 and 16 s), and the opening moon returns as the sunken moon later, so the film rhymes with itself. Everything is
on one dark grainy paper with a slight frame-to-frame boil, which makes cuts feel like page turns, not edits.

## Build it in our studio
- Engine: `canvas` (preferred). Paint 4-5 hero illustrations as layered PNGs (in-house or `gen-image`, paid, with
  approval), or use a Lemo-Opuscar painterly style for the textures.
- Scene plan: 1) get a timed word list from the client's voiceover or licensed track (`hyperframes transcribe` or
  `beats.py`); 2) draw the word layer as a hard swap at each word start; 3) per scene, one hero layer with a
  closed-form micro-cycle (moon phase as a circle masked by an offset ellipse; orbit as a point on a tilted
  ellipse with depth sorting against the hero; leaves scaled with `spring()` on stressed words); 4) a paper grain
  overlay and a 12 fps boil (seeded offset noise on the line layer); 5) a single push-in transition with planets
  as parallax layers flying past.
- Hard part: making the micro-cycles hit the words. Drive everything from the word timestamp table, never from
  free-running time.
- Render cost is low (2D layers). Difficulty 2, or 3 if the illustrations are hand-drawn from scratch.
