## The film in one line
Two cartoon turkeys meet in front of a columned building three times: with Japanese flags over their heads they
bow in sync, with American flags they hug and back-slap, and with one flag each the American lunges in for a
hug just as the Japanese one bows, and their heads collide.

## Signature device
The rule of three as a culture compare: beat 1 is "A meets A" (smooth), beat 2 is "B meets B" (smooth), beat 3 is
"A meets B" and the two smooth rituals cancel each other into a collision. Everything else is held constant so
only the ritual changes: same locked wide shot, same two characters on marks at about 20% and 80% of frame width,
same background. The label system is two icons floating above the heads (here flags, about 12% of frame width,
at shoulder height of the building) plus one caption line at top centre. Each beat is about 1.6 s: approach
(0.3 s), the ritual (0.8 s), reset (0.5 s). The third beat is cut short at the moment of impact, which is the
punchline and the loop point.

## Why it works
- Instant format literacy: two flags plus a greeting explain the setup in under a second.
- The third beat is predictable in shape but not in outcome, so the viewer stays for the payoff.
- Payoff at 4.5 s of 5: almost no tail, so the loop starts before attention drops.
- Comment trigger: people add their own country's greeting and argue about which is worse; share trigger for
  anyone who has lived between two cultures.
