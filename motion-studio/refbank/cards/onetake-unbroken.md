## The film in one line
On black, a thin rule splits the frame and an ivory brush starts the same circle on both sides, but on the left small notification chips keep popping up and each one lifts the brush, leaving a ring of 17 broken fragments, while the right side finishes one unbroken circle; the left half falls away, the camera pulls back, and the circle becomes the o in the product name, followed by a short tagline and a small red seal.

Craft reference, not a social post: a 16:9 film for a fictional notification-holding Mac app, from the onetake repo. Study only (PolyForm Noncommercial): ideas in our words, no code, text, pixels or sound reused.

## Why it stops the scroll (first 2 s)
- 0.0-0.5 s: black with faint vertical brush texture; a rule begins to draw down the middle.
- 1.0 s: tiny labels at the top of each half (the same time range; notifications on versus held).

Mechanism: a contrast split, the same action under two conditions, so the viewer waits to see which side wins. Very quiet open (hook change 0.01); the premise is only clear by about 3 s, when the first ping breaks the left stroke.

## Signature device
Before and after on a split screen with one shared clock. Both halves run the same timeline; only the left receives interruptions. Each interruption is a chip popping beside the stroke, a small shake applied to the left half only, and the brush lifting and resuming slightly behind, so strokes get shorter as pings quicken. The brush is procedural: dozens of fine bristle lines along the path with seeded dry-brush gaps, ivory on black. The finished circle is the o of the logo; a pull-back from 1x to about 0.15x turns the hero object into a letter.

## Build it in our studio
Engine: canvas (seeded procedural bristle strokes, path-length reveal, per-half shake). A Lemo-Opuscar ink style could supply the painterly layer if the brand suits it.
1. Split and labels.
2. One shared path for both halves.
3. An interruption schedule that quickens, each event breaking the left stroke.
4. Counter.
5. Hold, then drop the left half.
6. Pull back into the logo word and tagline.
Hard part: brushwork that looks hand-made at 60 fps (many thin bristles with pressure and dry gaps along the path, all seeded). Difficulty 3, zero assets.

9:16: split top and bottom instead of left and right; each half a band of about 1080 x 650 inside the safe zone; circles at about 45 % of width; labels at 44 px or more; the pull-back lands the logo in the middle band. Relevance 5: before and after is the most transferable business message there is.
