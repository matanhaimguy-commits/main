## Signature device
A real street object becomes a creature, using a face that was already on it. The box is about 1.6 m tall; the
googly eyes (each about a fifth of the box width) are visible from frame one as street art, so the first second
reads as normal. At 1.0 s the front panel becomes a mouth and a tongue about 3 m long unrolls out of it as a
soft body: glossy, saturated magenta with darker veins and a wet specular highlight, heavy at the tip so it slaps
and drags on the tiles. The eyes are also soft: they bulge, then pop out of their sockets as bouncy pink eyeballs
(with irises) that bounce three or four times across the floor and stay there, while new eyes appear in the
sockets. The cycle (tongue out, eyes pop, settle) repeats about every 3 to 4 s and the pile of eyeballs grows, so
the frame gets busier. Camera: handheld phone, walking in toward the box and swinging around it.

## Build it in our studio
- Engine: `canvas` over a locked or slowly moving plate; `gen-video` (paid, approval) for a photoreal handheld
  version.
- Scene plan: 1) film the client's object (vending machine, letterbox, shop door) locked off for 11 s with life
  around it; 2) in canvas, build the soft part as a verlet chain of 20 to 30 points with gravity, ground
  collision and spring stiffness, drawn as a thick shaded ribbon with a specular stripe; 3) eyeballs as circles
  with a closed-form bounce (damped `spring()` on height), shaded sphere and iris; 4) repeat the cycle every 3.5 s
  with seeded variation; 5) slap and boing SFX placed from the event times.
- Hard part: making a 2D soft body read as glossy 3D on real footage. Use a two-tone gradient across the ribbon,
  a moving highlight, a soft contact shadow on the floor, and slight motion blur from the renderer.
- Difficulty 3 for a locked-off plate; 4 or more for the handheld photoreal version.
