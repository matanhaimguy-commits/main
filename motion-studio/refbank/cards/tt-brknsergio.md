## The film in one line
A young man bounces on a backyard trampoline filmed from mat level; on every big jump a glowing portal opens in
the mat, he flips head first into it, and the cut lands on an anime hero in the same head-first dive falling
into that hero's world (four worlds in a row), each time snapping back to the trampoline as he rises out of
the fading portal.

## Signature device
A portal match cut on a dive pose. The camera lies on the trampoline mat, pointing up at about 20 degrees with
a slight wide lens, so the man fills the middle third against sky and the mat surface fills the bottom 30%.
The portal is an ellipse on the mat (about 70% of frame width), a different colour per world, that grows from a
spark in 0.5 s while he rises, then shows a preview of the destination inside it. At the top of the jump he
turns head first with arms spread; the cut lands on a 2D character in the identical silhouette and screen
position, falling the same direction, so the eye carries the motion across the cut. Returning uses a 0.5 s
radial blur from the world back to the yard, and the portal's sparkles linger in the world's colour as he rises.
Each cycle is 5 s: 1.5 s live bounce, 0.5 s portal dive, 2.5 s world, 0.5 s return.

## Why it works
- Familiar IP: four instantly recognised worlds, so viewers comment their favourite and request the next.
- List of N with a fixed ritual: viewers learn the bounce, portal, world rhythm by the second cycle and stay
  to see the next reveal.
- The match cut on pose is satisfying and rewatchable; saves (721k) come from fans of the shows.

## Build it in our studio
- Engine: client `footage` (locked low camera, person jumping or diving) composited with our own illustrated
  worlds from a `lemo:<style>` (for example watercolor or ink wash) or the `canvas` engine; portal and blur
  transitions in HyperFrames or canvas.
- Scene plan: 1) shoot 4 jumps from one locked camera; 2) portal: a growing ellipse on the mat, masked to the
  mat plane, filled with a still of the destination; 3) for each world, a 2.5 s illustrated fall with a
  character drawn in the same pose and position as the jump's top frame; 4) radial-blur return; 5) music with a
  whoosh on each dive.
- Hard part: drawing the falling figure so it matches the actor's silhouette. Trace the top-of-jump frame as
  the first key pose of each world.
- Difficulty 3: no tracking (locked camera), but four illustrated worlds take time.
