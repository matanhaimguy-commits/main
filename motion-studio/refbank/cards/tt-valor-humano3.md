## The film in one line
A title card promises four "useless organs", then a calm narrator spends the whole film on just one, the
tailbone: where it is, why we had tails, how they shrank, why it does nothing, and the comic pain of landing on
it, each sentence drawn as one flat picture on white.

  covered, so the other three are an open loop for the next posts.

## Signature device
Literal one-picture-per-sentence illustration on a white page, alternating three card types: (1) a flat
illustration that shows the noun or verb just spoken (monkey, spine, chair), often with one keyword in
condensed black caps above or below; (2) a stick figure (white circle head with a thick black outline, stick
limbs, black mitten hands and feet) acting out the human consequence in two or three poses; (3) a text-only
card in a rounded bold font, centred, used for the punchlines and rule-of-three lines. Red is the only accent
and only means "bad": X marks, "USELESS", impact bursts, pulse rings. Pictures change on every clause (a big
change every 1.35 s) while the narration stays slow and even.

## Why it works
- Script craft: short declarative sentences, a three-item list ("balance, climbing, gripping"), a three-beat
  negative ("connects to nothing, moves nothing, does nothing"), then a twist from "useless" to "the only time
  you notice it", which turns a fact into a relatable pain joke.
- Open loop by structure: the 4-tile grid promises more than the episode delivers, which drives follows and
  "do the appendix next" comments (7 k comments, breakout about 330 x followers).
- Calm piano under escalating pain comedy creates a dry tone.
