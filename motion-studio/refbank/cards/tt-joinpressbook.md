## The film in one line
A startup's marketer sits in a café with her chin on her hand, staring at something off camera under the caption
"thank god I work in marketing bc wtf is that", a passer-by pulls the same face ("even she agrees"), and a whip
pan reveals what they are looking at: the man at her table (a technical founder, going by the hashtags) typing dense
code on a laptop.

## Why it stops the scroll (first 2 s)
- 0.0 s: low-angle phone shot from across the table. Young woman in a white halter top with wired earphones,
  cheek on her hand, looking off to the right with a flat, unimpressed face. Warm café interior, round ceiling
  lights. Caption, centred in the upper third, three lines of white sans: "thank god I work in marketing bc
  wtf is that".
- 0.5-2.0 s: almost nothing changes. She blinks and keeps staring. Hook change in the first 2 s is only 0.116.
- Mechanism: a pure curiosity gap. The caption names a "that" the viewer cannot see, and her face says it is
  baffling. The joke lands for anyone who has sat next to a developer, so the still frame is enough.

## Signature device
The withheld reaction. For the first 5 s the camera only shows a face reacting to something out of frame, and the
caption names it vaguely ("that"). A stranger walking past reacts to the same unseen thing, which raises the
stakes with a second witness. Then one whip pan (about 0.3 s, heavy motion blur) carries the camera across the
table into a first-person shot of the "that": a laptop screen full of small, unreadable code, filmed close
enough that it fills the frame. The payoff is not a joke line but the image itself, which the viewer finds
exactly as baffling as she does. The caption never changes, so the reveal reads instantly with the sound off.

## Pacing read
10 cuts per 10 s and a big change every 0.69 s overstate the edit: there are really three shots (her, the
reframe with the passer-by, his POV) plus a whip and a handheld push, which the metric counts as many cuts. CV
0.78 shows the uneven rhythm: a long still opening (0 to 3.5 s near frozen, hook change 0.116), a small bump at
3.5 s, then a burst from 5.5 s. There is no true rest because the push into the screen keeps moving. It ends
mid-typing with no outro, so it loops back to her blank stare, which reads as her reaction to the code.

## Why it works
- Relatability for two audiences at once: marketers who do not code and developers who are proud of it. Both
  share it to the other.
- Curiosity gap held for 5.5 s with almost no motion, which is cheap and still works because the face carries it.
- Witness escalation: the passer-by makes it a shared verdict and adds a laugh beat.
- Loop: the ending cuts straight back to her blank face.
- Brand fit without selling: the account is the company itself, and the team dynamic is the product story.
  Breakout of about 550 times its 3.4k followers.

## Build it in our studio
- Engine: client footage (two phone shots and a whip pan, filmed by the client team) with HyperFrames for the
  caption and edit. Without footage, a stylised Claude Animation Base version with two characters (slow).
- Scene plan: 1) the client's non-technical person reacts to an unseen thing for 4 to 5 s; 2) a colleague walks
  past and pulls the same face, small second caption; 3) whip pan, 0.3 s, add directional blur in post if the
  phone did not; 4) POV push into the real thing (their dashboard, spreadsheet, tool, kitchen ticket rail);
  5) cut on a busy frame, no logo.
- Hard part: acting. The face must be deadpan and held; give the performer a real thing to look at.
- Difficulty 2: trivial edit, but we depend on the client filming it. Render cost negligible.
