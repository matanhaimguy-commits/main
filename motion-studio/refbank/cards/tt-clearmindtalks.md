## Signature device
One glowing pictogram per spoken phrase, on a fixed stage. The stage never changes: black field, a 1 px grey
ground line at about 70 percent height, wordmark under its left end. The figure is a white round-headed stick
person (head about 6 percent of frame width) with a soft white bloom, always standing on that line. Each new
idea swaps the prop next to the figure (bed, chair and papers, question mark, door, battery, hamster wheel,
leaves), while earlier props dim to grey before they go, so the eye always lands on the brightest thing.
Captions sit centred at about 30 percent height in bold condensed white title case, one to four words per
chunk, stacked up to three lines, changing every 1-1.5 s in step with the voice. Colour is used only twice:
the red low battery and the green leaves.

## Why it works
- The script is a tight arc: relatable claim, agitation ("you think you're resting"), a named concept
  ("Psychology calls it mental rumination"), reassurance ("not because you're lazy"), a turn ("But here's the
  truth"), a chiasmus anyone can quote ("You don't need to feel better to go outside. You need to go outside
  to feel better."), small actions, urgency, follow CTA.
- Second-person voice and a faceless figure let every viewer project onto it; that drives sends (589.8 K
  shares) and saves (435 K).
- Minimal visuals make it watchable with or without sound; the captions carry the message silently.
- Breakout of about 94 times the follower count.

## Build it in our studio
- Engine: `canvas` (pictograms drawn as strokes with a blur pass for the bloom), or `hyperframes` with SVG.
- Scene plan: 1) write the VO script in the same arc and record or generate the voice; 2) get word timings;
  3) build a library of 10-14 pictogram props in one stroke style and a figure rig with 6 poses; 4) place a
  prop per phrase, dim the outgoing prop over 0.3 s, fade the new one in; 5) captions chunked from the word
  timings; 6) end on the client's real logo and a follow or book line.
- Hard part: drawing enough clear pictograms in one consistent style. Solve with a small set of reusable
  primitives (door, window, battery, wheel, cloud) and pose-to-pose figure frames rather than full animation.
- Difficulty 2. Render cost is low in canvas (simple scenes, 60 fps).
