## The film in one line
A motion designer films himself looking unsure, captioned "How much should I charge for this?", then cuts to his
desk where a large monitor plays his concept animation of a music app's group-listening feature (a glowing green
pill becomes a session, friends join, songs are added to a shared queue) and it ends on the brand wordmark.

## Why it stops the scroll (first 2 s)
- 0.0 s: blurry, low-angle selfie of a young man in a white T-shirt, camera swinging. Caption in a black box,
  white sans, upper third: "How much should I charge for this?" (stays on screen for the whole film).
- 0.5-1.5 s: he rests his chin on his hand and looks down at the lens, a "thinking" pose, bedroom behind him.
- 2.0 s: whip blur as the phone swings away; 2.5 s hard cut to a hand on a laptop trackpad with an editing app
  open, a big monitor above glowing green.
- Mechanism: a money question every freelancer and client has an opinion on. The viewer is asked to judge the
  work, so they watch it to the end to form a price. The self-deprecating selfie makes it feel like a friend
  asking, not an ad.

## Signature device
Screen in scene. The product animation is never shown full frame. It plays on a 27 to 32 inch monitor filmed on a
phone from about 1.2 m away, so it fills only the middle third of the 9:16 frame (roughly 75% of the width, 30%
of the height), with the laptop running the edit software below it and a framed painting above it. The camera is
handheld and drifts slightly. This turns a polished UI concept into proof of craft: it looks like work on a real
desk, not a slick ad. The animation inside uses one object that keeps changing role: a rounded green pill on
black. It grows from a soft blob, becomes a labelled button, is clicked by a cursor, floods the screen with
ripple rings, shrinks into an avatar stack, splits into three buttons, and later becomes a plus, a check and a
list. Each state lasts about 1 to 1.5 s, and black backgrounds with one glowing accent read clearly even at small
size on a filmed monitor.

## Build it in our studio
- Engine: HyperFrames for the UI animation; client footage for the desk plate (or the client films their own
  monitor playing our render, which is the most honest version).
- Scene plan: 1) 2 s hook, filmed by the client on a phone, with a question caption about their own product;
  2) build the UI chain in HyperFrames from the client's real UI tokens: pill grows, label, cursor click, ripple
  rings, avatar stack, split into buttons, list build; 3) render 1:1 or 16:9 at the monitor's ratio; 4) the
  client plays it full screen and films the monitor handheld from about 1.2 m, or we place it into a desk photo
  with a perspective corner pin and a slow seeded drift in the canvas engine; 5) end on the client's logo.
- Hard part: the in-room screen must look filmed, not pasted. If we composite, add screen glow on the desk,
  slight moire-free softness and handheld drift; filming the real monitor avoids it entirely.
- Difficulty 2: the UI animation is standard HyperFrames work; render cost about 16 s per second for 20 s.
