## Signature device
A two-act "process then reveal" structure with one fixed question on top. Act 1 (0-17 s): roughly one-second
cuts of grey clay modelling in the software interface, each showing one product being formed from primitives
(spheres merged to a cloud, cylinders bevelled to a lipstick, a thread spiral on a gloss neck). The UI chrome
(timeline, toolbar, orange selection outlines) stays visible, so it reads as real work, not a trailer. The
headline sits fixed in the top third for the whole act. Act 2 (18-34 s): headline removed, UI removed, full
materials and light. A single recurring set (round mirror in an open scallop shell, sky reflected in the
mirror, water at the base) hosts each product in turn, each product given 3-4 s and one mechanical action (cap
lifts, wand pulls, lid opens). Every product repeats one motif from the modelling act (the cloud shape), so the
viewer recognises what they watched being built.

## Why it works
The "what if [celebrity] made [product]" premise is a share and comment trigger (fans tag friends, argue about
fit, ask to buy). The grey modelling act delays gratification for 17 s while building trust in the craft, and
the headline keeps the question open the whole time. The switch to colour at 18 s is a clear payoff that
rewards everyone who stayed. Each product then gets its own small reveal, so the second half is a list of mini
payoffs. The cloud motif links modelling and render, which invites a rewatch to match pieces. Saves (159k) show
it was kept as inspiration.

## Build it in our studio
Engine: canvas for the process half, client renders or generated stills for the reveal half.
1. Process act (canvas): draw the client's product as a wireframe and flat grey shading that builds step by
   step (outline draws on, fills appear, details snap on), with a plain software-like viewport grid. 1 s steps.
2. Fixed question headline in the top third, inside the safe zone.
3. Reveal act: the client's own product photos or renders, cut out and floated in one designed set (a backdrop
   in their colours), each with a slow push-in and one action (lid opens, cap lifts) faked with 2D layer
   separation.
4. End on the product range or one product opening; optional end card with the real logo.
- Hard part: photoreal 3D modelling and materials are outside our stack. Solve it with client-supplied renders
  or packshots; if none exist, the reveal half needs `gen-image` (paid, approval) for the set and product
  renders, which risks product inaccuracy. A stylised 2D version (flat illustrated products) is doable in
  canvas at difficulty 3.
- Render cost: canvas 60 fps, 35 s, cheap.
