## Signature device
A two-act "process, then reveal" film under one fixed question. Act 1 (0-17 s): one-second cuts of grey clay
modelling with the software UI visible (timeline, orange selection outlines), each step forming one product from
primitives, so it reads as real work. Act 2 (18-34 s): headline and UI removed, full materials and light. One
recurring set (round mirror in an open scallop shell, sky in the mirror, water at the base) hosts each product
for 3-4 s with one mechanical action (cap lifts, wand pulls, lid opens). The cloud motif from modelling repeats
in every product, so viewers recognise what they saw being built.

## Why it works
"What if [celebrity] made [product]" is a comment and tag trigger. The grey act delays gratification for 17 s
while proving craft, and the headline keeps the question open. The switch to colour pays everyone who stayed,
then each product is a small payoff of its own. 159k saves show it was kept as inspiration.

## Steal this (grammar) / Leave this (content)
- Steal: process act then reveal act; a fixed question headline that disappears at the reveal; one hero set for
  every product; one mechanical action per product; a shape motif shared across the range.
- Leave: the celebrity name, wordmark and song, the bow, pearl and cloud designs, the shell-mirror set. Never
  attach a real person's name to an invented product for a client.

## Build it in our studio
Engine: canvas for the process act; client renders or packshots for the reveal.
1. Process act: the client's product as wireframe and flat grey shading that builds in 1 s steps on a
   software-style grid, question headline fixed in the top third.
2. Reveal act: real product photos or renders cut out and floated in one designed set in brand colours, each
   with a slow push and one action faked with 2D layer separation.
3. End on the range or one product opening; real logo optional.
- Hard part: photoreal modelling is outside our stack. Use client renders; otherwise `gen-image` (paid,
  approval) risks product inaccuracy. A flat illustrated version is difficulty 3 in canvas.
