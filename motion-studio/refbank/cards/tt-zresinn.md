## The film in one line
A square lyric edit for a pop song: tartan, Big Ben, phone boxes, Union Jacks, glitter stars, lipstick kisses and
torn paper are cut into a new collage card for every word ("never", "liked", "When", "you'd do", "LET it ALL
GO", "DO me LIKE"), one card roughly every half second, and the chorus section plays twice so the edit loops.

## Signature device
One lyric word, one collage card. Each card is a square composition of 3 to 6 layers: a fabric or paper ground
(red, blue or green tartan, grid paper, torn white paper edge), one cut-out photo element (Big Ben, a phone box,
a portrait in blue duotone), one or two small stickers (glitter star, lipstick kiss, checkerboard strip, doodled
crown), and the sung word set large, about 40 to 60% of the frame width, in a type style that changes per word:
rounded bold on a pill, red sticker, outlined script, ransom-note cut letters, condensed poster type, tartan-filled
outline letters. Cards switch on the syllable, about every 0.5 s, with occasional in-card motion (a word stacking
under the last, a banner sliding up, a defocus). The whole 6.5 s chorus block is reused for the repeat, so the
edit is built once and looped.

## Look
- Palette by role: charcoal and black grounds (#2c2b2c), navy tartan (#3b4a6b), paper white (#e2e2e0), warm greys
  (#8e8280), oxblood red (#7e3a35), with green tartan and glitter accents.
- Type: a different face per word: heavy rounded sans in a pill, red sticker type, outlined script, ransom-note
  cut letters, condensed grotesk, stencil-like distressed caps. All very large, the lyric always the biggest
  element. Words sit in the middle third.
- Texture: scanned fabric, torn paper, glitter, grain, halftone and duotone portraits, slight print blur.
- Camera: none; flat square cards with small drifts and rotations.

## Why it works
- Song recognition plus lyrics on screen invites singing along and rewatching (57.6k saves, 3.2% of plays).
- Density rewards pausing: every card has details, so viewers rewatch to see them.
- A clear theme (British punk scrapbook) makes the chaos feel designed.
- The repeated chorus block makes the loop seamless.

## Build it in our studio
- Engine: the collage route (paper-cutout and halftone look) assembled in HyperFrames; beats and word times
  from `beats.py` and a transcription of the client's licensed or generated track or voiceover line.
- Scene plan: 1) pick a theme kit from the client's world (their textures, product cut-outs, real photos);
  2) design 6 to 8 card templates with fixed layer slots; 3) one card per word on the word timings;
  4) add a single in-card move per card (stack, slide, defocus); 5) reuse the block for the repeat and the loop.
- Hard part: producing many distinct, good-looking cards. Solve with templates plus procedural textures (tartan,
  grid paper, torn edges, halftone) and a strict palette, and run the contact sheet to catch weak cards.
- Difficulty 3: no hard simulation, but a lot of design work; render about 16 s per second in HyperFrames.

## Business translations
- Fashion e-commerce: the brand's campaign slogan, one word per card, built from scans of its own fabrics and

- Music venue or festival: the night's tagline word by word over collages of the real lineup's approved photos,
  cut to a licensed track.
- Restaurant: "fresh, loud, late" style menu words on cards made of real menu paper, ingredient cut-outs and the
