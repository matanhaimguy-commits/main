## The film in one line
A neon-blue man sits beside a ticking orange clock as a voice asks why smart people leave work to the last minute.
About 30 glowing x-ray pictures (brains, neurons, a red alarm clock, molecules, a hero) then pass one per phrase,
each under a single spoken word, and the film ends back on the man at his keyboard.

## Signature device
One literal picture per phrase and one word per beat. Every 1.5-2 s a new full-frame picture appears, all blue x-ray
and hologram line art on near-black. Each shows its phrase's noun: a clock for "last minute", a spiking chart for
"significantly more", a red alarm clock for "deadline". The VO appears one word at a time, centred, in heavy
uppercase sans. Words are white, with an orange fill that seems to sweep across the letters ("SIGNIFICANT" white,
"LY" orange). Colour carries the argument: blue is calm, red is panic, orange is payoff. Each picture slowly pushes
in, and the last shot repeats the first.

## Why it works
- Identity flattery: procrastinators share it to justify themselves (123.7K shares, 2.2% of plays, about 300 times
  the follower count).
- Two open loops push the payoff later.
- A named concept gives people something to save and repeat (68.9K saves).
- The reframe turns guilt into permission, which drives comments.
- It reads with the sound off, and the bookend makes it loop.

- Plan: 1) a sourced script in this ladder; 2) VO and word timings; 3) one noun per phrase, grouped into 6-8

  6) one-word captions with keyword fill.
- Hard part: 30 matching pictures. Use families (one brain or clock in different states) and a counter-ticker,
  not a particle burst, for the number.
- Render: about 16 minutes. Difficulty 3 procedural, 2 with gen-image.
