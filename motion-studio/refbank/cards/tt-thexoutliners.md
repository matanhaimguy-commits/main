## The film in one line
Game-style 3D characters act out the 1971 hijacking sentence by sentence: a man in a grey suit shows a
stewardess a bomb in his briefcase, collects money and parachutes on a runway, locks the crew in the cockpit,
walks to an open hatch and jumps into the clouds, and searchers with a dog comb a night forest for him.

Script structure: superlative hook naming the payoff ("unsolved") > dateline with full date and name > inciting
act (the bomb) > demands with an understatement ("very simple") and two exact numbers > three escalation steps
in plain past tense > a mid-story open loop (he and the bomb are "nowhere to be found", stairs left down) >
the answer one clause later ("because ... jumped") > unresolved ending that repeats the hook word "unsolved",
so the last line rhymes with the first. About 150 words in 60 s, every sentence gets its own literal shot.

## Signature device
Literal reenactment: one rendered shot per clause of narration, cut exactly on the clause. Nouns become props
on a neutral void when they matter (money cube, parachute stack), actions become follow shots behind the
character, and the climax is a dolly-follow behind him to an open hatch, then a hold on the empty opening.
Captions are 1-3 words, bold caps, yellow with a dark outline, centred at about 80% of frame height.

## Why it works
- Bold claim plus the word "unsolved" opens a loop that the last line deliberately does not close.
- Concrete specifics (date, $200,000, four parachutes, Seattle, Reno) make it feel factual and learnable.
- Literal visuals mean the film works with the sound off, read through captions.
- Comment trigger: viewers post their own theories.
