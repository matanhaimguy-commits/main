## 2. Persona card (the cold readers' and the hook graders' only input besides the reel)
Copied from the song's `script-lab.md` section 2, with one line added (the starting belief for this step, in general
words; nothing about the cause or the fix):

You run a founder-led expert business: a consultant, coach or small agency owner, late 30s to mid 40s, English-speaking,
about $20K to $50K a month, working from home. Your best clients came by referral and you like them. It's late evening;
you're on the couch, phone in hand, scrolling Instagram Reels about business growth, sound off at first. A reel starts
playing in your feed. What you believe, in your words: "some months we'd sign 3 clients, other months literally
nothing"; "LinkedIn works for me, but it's sporadic and not predictable"; you think you need a better channel. **When a
prospect tells you they're also talking to other consultants, you think the answer is a sharper price, a bigger package
or a nicer proposal, and you've given a discount to win a client before.** What you've tried: cold email at higher
volume, LinkedIn, a coach, some ads; each helped for a while. What you don't know: how the separate parts of getting
clients fit together. Your doubts: you're tired of "post more", "more outreach", "fix your offer", and you distrust
anything that sounds like an agency pitch or "done-for-you". You give a reel 2 to 3 seconds and keep watching only
while you have a question you want answered and nothing confuses you. Would you send it, save it or follow?

## 4. Belief ladder (spine B, the recommended one) and the because test
| Rung | Belief in her head | Installed by (line in the release candidate) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Recognition | "I've had that message: went with someone cheaper." | L1, the hook |
| 2 Stakes (the accommodation) | "When they ask why me, I take something off, add a bonus, polish the proposal." | L2, L3 |
| 3 Release | "Those fixes came too late; it wasn't my price or my proposal." | L4 |
| 4 Aha | "My offer says what theirs say, so price was all they could compare." | L5, L6 (THE PICTURE: the same cloth) |
| 6 Mechanism | "Another owner's offer was cut from her buyers' words: their sentence first, then her own named steps." | L7 to L11 (the carrier L7 and L8, the bridge L9, the parts L10 and L11) |
| (5 Hope, folded) | "Then price stops being the only thing to compare." | L12 (said after the mechanism: the pair shows it, see the because note) |
| 8 Payoff (process) | "Her prospect asked about her steps, not her price; no yes yet, but nobody asked about price." | L13 to L15, mirroring L1, L2 and L8 |
| CTA | "This is a series about how the pieces fit; I want the next piece." | L16, L17 |

Because sentences:
- 1 to 2: Because she has had "went with someone cheaper", she recognises the question before it and her own answer.
- 2 to 3: Because she watched herself discount and polish, she can hear "too late" without blame: it was decided earlier.
- 3 to 4: Because the decision came earlier, she is ready for where: the offer she wrote, which says what theirs say.
- 4 to 6: Because "the same cloth" leaves only price, she wants to see an offer that is not the same cloth; the second
  consultant gets the same question and does not touch her price.
- 6 to 5: Because she has seen what the other offer is made of (the buyers' sentence, her own named steps), she can
  believe that price stopped being the only thing to compare. (Fixed point "promise before mechanism": in a two_days
  story the promise rides in the contrast "She didn't touch her price" (L8), said before the mechanism; L12 states it
  after, as in a story.)
- 5 to 8: Because price is no longer all there is, her prospect's question about the steps reads as true, not neat.
- 8 to CTA: Because she sees this is one piece of how getting clients fits together (what she doesn't know), she
  follows for the next piece.
Fixed points: cause (L4 to L6) before the mechanism (L9 to L11); explanation before the claim (L12); the brand once,
on the end card. No proof, offer or guarantee rungs.

## 6. Script bible
- **Speakers:** Voice 1, the narrator, in every line. "You" is the viewer, played by the mustard-sleeve hands (the POV
  camera); "she" and "her" are the second consultant (navy sleeve), introduced in L7 as "another consultant"; "your
  prospect" and "her own prospect" are two different people (loop 1's break). Quotes are framed by "You got:" and "Her
  prospect wrote:".
- **Timeline:** one week before (L2, L3, the question and the fixes), "when you wrote your offer" (months before, L4),
  "the same week" (L7, the second consultant's question), "that night" (L13 to L15, both answers). The hook (L1) is the
  end of the week, told first.
- **Picture map:** an offer = the page that describes what she sells (shown, "MY PROGRAM") · three offers that say the
  same = three swatches cut from one roll of plain cloth · the prospect's choice = the hand that picks the smallest
  price tag · the buyers' words = a cloth printed with large handwritten buyer phrases (SOME MONTHS NOTHING, FEAST OR
  FAMINE, SPORADIC: Q-R-0058, Q-R-0094, Q-R-0022, as in the song) · her offer = a piece cut from that cloth · her own
  steps = numbered steps under her own name on her page. The fixes (discount, BONUS page, glossy cover) are real props,
  never cloth, so the cloth means only "what the offer is made of".
- **Word list:** "offer" (explained in L4: "how you describe what you sell") · "the same cloth" (L6, the idiom, shown) ·
  "her buyers' own words" (L9, explained in L10) · "steps she named herself" (L11) · "part three", "the
  pieces" (L16; the series, explained by the line itself).
- **Setups and payoffs:** "Went with someone cheaper" (L1 picture, paid L13) · "why you, and not the others?" (L2, paid
  by the other prospect's question in L14) · "She didn't touch her price" (L8) paid by "nobody asked about price" (L15) · "took something off" (L3) paid by "She didn't touch her price" (L8) ·
  "the same cloth" (L6) paid by "cut from her buyers' own words" (L9).
- **Facts:** none. The two consultants and both prospects are composites in a labelled POV dramatization. No numbers.

**Writer's recommendation: spine B.** It opens on the strongest hook of the run (the only stack every grader read as
"that's me" at once), it carries the load-bearing belief (the deal was decided by the offer, not the price) as a
hidden cause, and it keeps the two futures of the template for the second half, where the pair has room to read on a
phone. A is the template in its pure form but its frame 1 lost the visual in all five hook rounds; C reads as advice.
