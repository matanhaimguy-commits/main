I've never said this out loud, not even to my accountant. I have three online stores built and sitting in the admin, and I haven't done anything with them in years.

Anyway. I should back up.

"I had one before," I would tell people later. "I just ended it because I was dealing with a lot."

The second store I built in a weekend, full of good intentions. It never had a single product loaded.

For the third, I did what everyone says to do. I asked about hiring someone.

I got three quotes. $5,000. $20,000. $15,000.

I'm like, seriously? Come on.

I would not pay $2,500 to anybody unless I could see some results. Let alone twenty thousand.

What I actually wanted was someone to fully automate my online business. I would have paid for that. Just not twenty thousand dollars of it, up front, on a promise.

I added it up once. Three stores. The monthly fees. A theme I paid for twice. The weekends I will never get back. I stopped adding.

Some nights I would open the admin, look at the three store names, and close it again without clicking anything. It felt like opening a drawer of bills.

What I told myself was always the same. I'm not a computer person. I don't have the time. I can't compete with the big stores anyway.

None of that turned out to be the reason.

I was moving the scarves to the front because they had sold. I was marking down a jacket that hadn't moved in six weeks. I was changing the window. And somewhere between the price gun and the mannequin, I realized I had done this every single week for eleven years.

That's the job. Opening day isn't the job. The job is every week after it. See what sells, move it forward, take out what doesn't, bring in the next thing.

I would never open up once, flip the sign, and walk away for three years.

That is exactly what happened to all three of my stores.

A listing gets no attention at all.

It wasn't the platform, and it wasn't me. A built store is not a running store, and nobody had the job of running mine.

Then the part most companies bury. Product costs, platform fees and ad spend are yours, not in the $500. The ongoing work is quoted on the call before you commit. No income guarantees. I trusted that more than a promise.

Relief, mostly. Like someone had finally walked into a room I had left the lights on in for three years.

If I could tell the owner I was that night at 6:40 one thing, it would be this. You didn't fail at building stores. You built three. Nobody ever had the job of working them after they went live.

Every month mine sat, I paid for stores that did nothing. The $500 Build is what I started with, in my name, in stages, on a budget I approve. And they stay after launch day.

The receipt with the two lines is still on my desk.
