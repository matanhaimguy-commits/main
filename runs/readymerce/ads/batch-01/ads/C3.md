"Nobody quits to start one," the guy from purchasing said in the break room. He wasn't even talking to me.

My wife knew. Nobody at work did.

Same spot, same thermos, same sentence.

I was forty-one. I figured I'd be saying it in that parking spot at fifty-one.

I earn good money. Not rich. Good, with benefits I actually need. And every plan I ever made for the store hit the same wall: I don't want to quit and then start struggling.

My store was one truckload of one guess.

Somebody with a full-time job can do the build once. After that he gets one pass through the loop, maybe, in leftover hours. When that one guess misses, it looks like the idea failed. It didn't. One guess only proved one guess missed.
