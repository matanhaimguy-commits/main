## 3 · DEFINITIONS (only the ones this step uses — verbatim where the source matters)

1. **Big Domino.** "The ONE THING you need to get prospective followers to believe," which, if believed, makes "all their other objections disappear." Template: "[new opportunity / mechanism] is the key to [what you desire the most]." One per brand; the desire is the leaderboard's #1 in its "I want to…" wording with its LF8 root. A domino built on a functional outcome or a product result is a feature claim.
2. **First belief.** The avatar's own instance of the domino, in his words: "if [M-1 cause] is what has been happening, then [M-2] is how I finally get [his deeper hope]." One per launch avatar, so every avatar-adset carries its own domino.
3. **The seven belief classes.** CAUSE-BLAMED (what he blames now), CAUSE-INSTALLED (M-1), SOLUTION-BREAK (must break), SOLUTION-LEVERAGE (we ride it), VEHICLE ("not the right vehicle for me"), INTERNAL ("right for some people, but I could not make it work"), EXTERNAL ("an outside force holds me back"). VEHICLE breaks with case-study or demonstration proof; INTERNAL and EXTERNAL with an epiphany story and "people like me" proof. An unaddressed INTERNAL belief is the classic leak.
4. **Current, target, intervention, live_wire, THE ONE BELIEF.** `current` is "I believe…" in his verbatim words traced to a Q-##; `target` is the empowering belief in plain words; `intervention` is what we SHOW, never what we say. `live_wire: YES` marks a belief honored and never contradicted (pride, hard-won distrust, "I've tried everything") — the target is built beside it; `live_wire: NO` may serve as an opening. Exactly one B-## per avatar carries `the_one_belief: true`: the belief that must break before he buys.
5. **BELIEF-PLAIN.** A belief is not a mechanism ("the cause is something specific, not just my age" is a belief; "the disc closes on the nerve like a door" is a mechanism), and a belief is execution-agnostic (tied to one image, it is a draft of an ad). No coined term, handle, metaphor, enemy name or brand name inside a belief string.
6. **Gradualization.** "The art of starting your ad with a statement that will be immediately and entirely accepted, and then building a subsequent chain of acceptances upon this first statement." One believable statement earns the right to make the next; the mechanism should feel like the natural answer to a question the copy already created. Build the **solution requirement** before the reveal — he should almost predict what the solution must do before hearing the product name. Eight route architectures: pure causal chain; contradiction (the old explanation cannot explain an observation); yes-but-incomplete (true diagnosis, missing second layer); failed-solution (what she tried performs a different job); two-problem (one resolves, another remains); pattern-discovery (repeated cases share a variable); comparison (similar people, different outcomes); delayed-effect (triggered earlier than it shows). Chain format: `X happens. → X affects Y. → Y matters because… → When Y changes… → That explains Z. → Therefore the solution needs to… → [product]`. **Biggest Leap** = the one sentence naming the hardest belief to establish.
7. **Lane pass (per avatar).** Step 10 chose the market lane (New Mechanism | New Information | New Identity) from the dominant stage and deferred the per-avatar pass. Here each avatar's `local_stage` and `awareness_rung` (09) set `lane_applied` and the entry his chain opens on: a stage-5 avatar in a stage-3 market opens on identity or new information, never on the mechanism claim; an unaware avatar opens on the lived moment, never on the problem's market name.
8. **Belief sequence (backward chain).** Fix the destination (the final buying belief), then the starting point (his Current beliefs), then derive the order backward — what must he believe just before the final belief? and before that? — until a belief he already holds (`live_wire: NO`). That bottom belief is THE OPENING. Present it forward as 3–6 plain paragraphs. Violate a belief he already holds, even slightly, and nothing else can save the ad.
9. **New opportunity, not improvement.** "That thing you tried that failed wasn't your fault" — the vehicle was wrong, not him. An improvement offer re-triggers the memory of failure; a new opportunity gives new hope.
10. **Objection, "even if", the four forces.** Written as a sceptical Redditor says it; answered as "[benefit] even if [objection]." Classes: money, effort, time, safety, fear of failure / disappointment / embarrassment / gullibility / dependence, identity-based, fits-my-case, timeline, another-routine. A switch happens only when Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit; anxiety is "the most underestimated force," habit is "why great products lose." Every objection is tagged ANXIETY, HABIT or NULL.
11. **Failed solution.** A tried solution: `solution_status` ∈ {TRIED-FAILED, TRIED-HELPED-THEN-RETURNED, STOPPED-SIDE-EFFECTS}. "Mentioned" ≠ "tried"; considered and recommended-by-others route to objections (a category rule, not a floor). The reported result and the customer's theory of why are stored separately.
12. **Proof.** Types: owned_review, owned_demo, shootable_demo, product_truth_pattern, curated_review, external_mechanism_evidence, data_stat, authority, guarantee, founder_note, outside_observer, timeline, first100. Status: EXISTS | SHOOTABLE-NOW | PLANNED. Default rank: demonstration > case/real results > data > authority > outside observer > timeline > guarantee > volume > media, re-ordered per avatar by what the corpus says he trusts. Stacked proof beats single proof; authority is an accelerant, never the spine; the guarantee closes residual fear, never opens. Provenance hierarchy: owned proof → owned demo → exact-SKU product truth → external mechanism evidence → market VOC as language. A testimonial assembled from real VOC lines carries `SYNTHESIZED` with its Q-##; a credentialed voice carries `AUTHORITY` with the authority read beside it — tags the team reads, never removals. Real reviews are curated by selection, ordering, trimming and typo fixes.
13. **Basis (external evidence).** `basis: cited | competitor-claimed | category-typical | inferred`, plus a `note`: what the source-read found (opens at its URL; same population; same outcome; comparable dose or form; effect direction) and any gap on the six honesty checks (association vs cause · animal or in-vitro vs human · component vs finished product · mechanism vs outcome · one trial vs consensus · self-report vs measured). Basis describes and tiebreaks; it never removes a claim.
14. **Skepticism reasons (eight).** sounds too simple, tried too many things, wasted too much money, heard too many promises, nearly ignores the recommendation, thinks the recommender exaggerates, doubts the problem is the same, does not want another product/routine. Each pairs with a self-protective narrative and a disarm device.
15. **Existing Knowledge & Beliefs (EK).** What strong advertisers assume the prospect already knows, believes, has been told, has tried, expects, distrusts or understands without explanation — the starting point of the sales argument. Keep apart what is OBJECTIVELY TRUE, what the CUSTOMER appears to BELIEVE and what the ADVERTISER ASSUMES.
16. **Warren's five.** "People will do anything for those who encourage their dreams, justify their failures, allay their fears, confirm their suspicions and help them throw rocks at their enemies." Validate, never correct.
17. **Epiphany Bridge (eight beats).** backstory → desire (external + internal) → the wall → the epiphany → the plan → conflict → achievement → transformation.
18. **Honest timeline, Honest Box.** Timeline = first sign / full effect as buyer-reported PT-## ranges, never an overnight transformation. Honest Box = `Best for X. If Y is your main need, this may not be the right fit.` built from the top recurring first-use complaint → limitation → who it is NOT for → remedy line.
19. **The five buying beliefs.** BB-I I trust this brand; BB-II my problem CAN be solved; BB-III THIS solves it; BB-IV better than the alternatives; BB-V now, not later. Every B-## is an instance under one of them.
20. **Recurrence labels** (in code from the four counters): VALIDATED = ≥3 unique authors across ≥2 independent threads; SUPPORTED = 2 authors, or 1 author + the belief attacked or assumed in ≥2 independent IM-## networks; EARLY SIGNAL = 1 author (upvotes noted, never a substitute); HYPOTHESIS = analyst-derived `[D]`; CONTESTED = contradicted by ≥3 authors with no supporting side (still written; feeds DNS-##). REJECTED only for a category error (an objection or a mechanism sentence minted as a belief).
21. **Evidence tiers on quotes:** `[R-PAGE]` fetched page, `[R-SCRAPE]` scraped dataset, `[R-PUB]` published source, `[R-OWNED]` operator-owned data, `[R-SNIPPET]` search snippet (counts toward no floor; a Current belief traced only to a snippet prints its tier and HYPOTHESIS).

## 6 · VALIDATION — what earns each label

1. **Domino test.** All T1 dissolved → `DOMINO: PASS`; any T1 survives → `DOMINO: PARTIAL` with survivors and a `mechanism_note` to Step 10.
2. **Desire anchoring.** BD-1 names a leaderboard MD-## with an `lf8_root`; a domino on a functional outcome or product result is a feature claim → rewritten from the DS-## ladder.
3. **Verbatim test.** Every `current` traces to ≥1 Q-## at `[R-PAGE]` / `[R-SCRAPE]`; no Q-## → `CURRENT: GAP — searched:`, HYPOTHESIS, priority printed beside it.
4. **Recurrence labels** (in code, per §3.20). The label describes the evidence; the priority is the analyst's read of what breaks the sale; both print on every row.
5. **Coverage test.** Per avatar VEHICLE, INTERNAL and EXTERNAL each have a breaker (a P-## or an epiphany beat); a missing INTERNAL prints `LEAK: INTERNAL — "works for them, not me" unanswered — AV-#`.
6. **Order test.** Statement 1 of every BS-AV# and GO-AV# is a held belief (Q-## + `live_wire: NO`); each later step rests on the one before; the solution requirement precedes the product name; a jump prints `BIGGEST LEAP`; no opening → `OPENING: GAP — searched:`.
7. **Lane-pass test.** `lane_applied` names the avatar's own stage and rung and differs from the market lane wherever 09's `local_stage` differs from 07's dominant stage; an opening that names the problem's market name to an unaware avatar → rewritten from his lived moment.
8. **Live-wire rule.** A `live_wire: YES` belief is honored in every target and intervention; a target that negates it is rewritten beside it.
9. **Tried-only (category, §3.11).** "considering" and "recommended by others only" route to objections.
10. **Elimination completeness.** M-1 explains every FS-## with ≥2 authors; an uncovered one prints `ELIMINATION: INCOMPLETE — FS-##`.
11. **Not-your-fault test.** Each `why_this_is_different` keeps the prospect's status (the vehicle was wrong, not him); blaming his effort or making the old solution sound stupid → rewritten.
12. **Proof-stack test.** Every P0 belief has ≥2 proof types with ≥1 at EXISTS or SHOOTABLE-NOW; demonstration ranks first wherever the Truth Card lists something visibly demonstrable; a P0 on PLANNED proof only prints `LEAK: P0 proof PLANNED — B-##` with its cost to close.
13. **Basis notes.** An unopened source prints `NOT OPENED — url` with `basis: competitor-claimed` or `inferred` per its origin; ranking is persuasive criteria first, basis second.
14. **Objection tests.** No prospect words → `VERBATIM: GAP`, HYPOTHESIS, `even_if_answer` marked `[AUTHORED, CHECK THIS]`; `jtbd_force` tagged; `answered_by` filled or UNRESOLVED; an unresolved T1 prints `LEAK: ATC→purchase — OBJ-##`.
15. **Warren test.** All five rows per avatar; a missing `failures_justified` prints as the named gap.
16. **BELIEF-PLAIN lint.** Nothing left after the strip → the belief was a draft of an ad → rewritten from its Q-##.
