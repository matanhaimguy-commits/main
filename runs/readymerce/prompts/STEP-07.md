## 3 · DEFINITIONS (only the ones this step uses)

**Awareness is a person; sophistication is a crowd.** Sophistication is diagnosed, never tested: "You don't test different levels of sophistication. Your sophistication just exists."

**The five awareness stages (print verbatim at the top of the output file; every tag is audited against them):**

* `AW-1 Unaware` — "Prospect doesn't recognize a problem or need. They don't identify with having the specific problem, but might identify with symptoms of it." Dropshipping reading: the unaware person may still identify with a symptom or a lived moment; that is the only door in.
* `AW-2 Problem Aware` — "Prospect knows they have a problem but not that a solution exists."
* `AW-3 Solution Aware` — "Prospect knows solutions that solve it, but not your product category or product type as a solution."
* `AW-4 Product Aware` — "Prospect knows your product category/type, but doesn't know why your product specifically is the best one."
* `AW-5 Most Aware` — "Prospect knows existing brands by brand name who sell our product category or exact product, but hasn't seen the right offer to buy." Dropshipping reframe: Most Aware means knowledge of existing brands selling our exact product type, never "knows our brand"; a new brand has no name anyone knows.

**Tag rule:** a record carries "the highest level demonstrated by a passage, not the person's complete knowledge." A tag exists only when the record's own text places it (names the problem, a category, a brand, or only a symptom); the default is NULL. Competitor knowledge establishes awareness of the product TYPE only under the `reference_product` line. A past purchase or review alone is not a CURRENT Most-Aware state; present-tense and retrospective awareness are counted apart.

**The five sophistication stages and their levers:** `S1` first to market → a simple, direct benefit statement; `S2` competition emerges → enlarge the claim to its limit (superlatives, quantifiers); `S3` saturation, customers skeptical of repeated promises → a new mechanism explaining HOW it works differently, WHAT becomes HOW; `S4` mechanism competition → enlarge the mechanism: easier, quicker, surer, solves more, overcomes old limitations; `S5` complete sophistication, "Customers actively resist all claims, seeking reasons NOT to buy" → "the emphasis shifts from the promise and the mechanism which accomplishes it, to identification with the prospect himself." Prior, printed never assumed: most e-commerce categories sit at S3–S5; a population or market can sit at S2–3 inside an S5 category (§4 item 9).

**Lever codes (the 10-minute ad-library method, made countable):** the read runs on the 20 LONGEST-RUNNING ads (controls, not bets), each tagged on the dominant element of headline + first two lines (any named mechanism → MECHANISM or EFS): `CLAIM` = outcome, no how, no quantifier; `ENLARGED-CLAIM` = quantified, superlative or time-bound; `MECHANISM` = names a cause or a how; `EFS` = easier-faster-surer, a mechanism enlarged ("even if…", "without…", "95% vs 20%"); `IDENTITY` = who the buyer is, the tribe, the enemy, no promise in the lead. Stage map: CLAIM→S1, ENLARGED-CLAIM→S2, MECHANISM→S3, EFS→S4, IDENTITY→S5. The majority lever is the current stage; "You must be one stage ahead, not five."

**The strategic responses to the crowd:** NEW MECHANISM (a new process, technology or delivery system that makes familiar promises fresh; needs demonstration and proof); NEW INFORMATION (a problem or cause they did not know, why previous mechanisms failed); NEW IDENTITY (WHO customers become rather than what products do); UNSERVED AVATAR (a population no network addresses). Each passes a capability check against `PT-##`; a response the product cannot deliver prints NOT CARRIED and stays in the table.

**The external prior:** the Larger Market Formula — 3% buying now, 17% gathering information, 20% problem-aware but not researching, 60% unaware. It prints BESIDE the counted distribution marked PRIOR; it is never our figure.

**Two proxies, three entry-gap shapes:** proxy 1 = corpus share of stage-tagged records; proxy 2 = incumbents' entry-stage share of opened ads. Buyers AW-2 while ads enter AW-3 = **entry gap (enter earlier)**; ads enter AW-1 while the corpus sits AW-3 = **incumbents educating a pool we can skip**; both agree = **crowded entry; differentiate on lever or population**.

## 6 · VALIDATION — what earns each label

1. **Stage tags.** A tag counts only when the record's own text places it; a purchaser source (`is_purchaser_source: Y`) does not by itself place a record at AW-5; a tag the text does not carry is set to NULL and the record stays in `N_total`.
2. **Awareness verdict.** DOMINANT = VALIDATED; PLURALITY = SUPPORTED; MIXED = EARLY SIGNAL (12 tests both stages); PROVISIONAL = HYPOTHESIS until 06 closes the `HARVEST-##` order.
3. **Derived distribution.** Never evidence on its own; it prints only with the `[D] SAMPLE(N)` label beside the raw counts.
4. **Sophistication.** CONFIRMED (≥60% one lever, ≥3 networks, no network >50% of rows) = VALIDATED; PLURALITY (40–59%) = SUPPORTED; MIXED (<40%; the higher stage governs the lever) = EARLY SIGNAL; <12 qualifying ads or <3 networks = HYPOTHESIS stamped `SOPH: PROVISIONAL (n=…)`. Rows under 14 days stay out of the count (bets, not controls).
5. **Longevity is evidence of the LEVER, not of the creative.** An `LTV-RICH` row counts but is flagged; LTV-RICH rows above 50% of the 20 cap the read at SUPPORTED.
6. **Sophistication is diagnosed, never tested, never inferred from failed purchases alone.** A market stage never comes from one population's awareness; a `local_stage` needs ≥5 addressed ads.
