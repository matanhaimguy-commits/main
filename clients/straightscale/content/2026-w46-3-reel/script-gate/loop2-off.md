## Answers

**1. Retelling from the picture, hook, supers and end card only (no captions):**
I was about to switch off my ad because I thought it didn't work, but the reel asks whether anyone actually answered the messages it brought. It says the ad's job ends when the message arrives, and then the message sits on a phone next to someone busy working, with no answer, no follow-up and no next step. If they waited, nobody owned the message, so I shouldn't switch the ad off yet.

**2. What the captions add:**
They turn it into something I can actually do: "Para se ta fikësh, shiko çfarë ndodhi me çdo mesazh" (before you switch it off, look at what happened with every message). They also explain "Ndjekje": did anyone follow up when the client went quiet. That one hits hardest, because we never do. And "Por ti je në punë, dhe mesazhi pret" (but you're at work, and the message waits) says out loud what the workbench shot only showed. So the meaning doesn't change, but it becomes a check I can do tomorrow morning, and the blame feels fairer: I'm busy, not lazy.

**3. Works, doesn't work, or neither?**
Mostly **neither**. It says the ad has one job, "Puna e reklamës: mesazhi," and that the real question is what happened after: "Nëse pritën: mesazhi s'kishte zot," which is conditional. But the opening line, "Reklama solli mesazhe," states as fact that the ad brought messages. That slightly assumes my ad works, and as a sceptic I notice it. "Mine brought messages? Some, maybe."

**4. Numbers, platforms, logos, prices, promises:**
None. There are no numbers, no Facebook or Instagram name or logo (just "REKLAMË"), and no price. The only brand is the StraightScale wordmark at the end. No text promises speed, guarantees anything or pushes urgency. The red unread dots and the sagging bubble give a quiet visual sense that time is passing, but they promise nothing. That's partly why I didn't feel sold to.

**5. Would I send it?**
**Yes**, but to my business partner or my sales guy rather than a friend. The reason: it doesn't ask me to pay anyone. It gives me one thing to go and check ("what happened to each message?"), and the workbench shot is exactly us.

**6. Most confusing moment:**
**13.0-16.5, when the ad card "stops, its part done."** A minute earlier I saw a finger on the off switch, so when the card goes still my first thought is "they switched it off." That's the opposite of the point. The thread ending at the card's edge is too subtle to see on a phone at 10 pm. Only the super "Puna e reklamës: mesazhi" tells me the card stopping means "job done," not "turned off."
