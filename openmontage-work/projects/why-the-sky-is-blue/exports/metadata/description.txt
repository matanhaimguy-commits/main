Sunlight is white and space is black, so why is the sky blue? In under a minute: how tiny air molecules scatter short blue waves about five times more than red ones, why the sky isn't violet, and why sunsets turn red.

What you'll learn:
• White sunlight is every color, each a wave of a different length
• Nitrogen and oxygen molecules scatter short (blue) waves far more than long (red) ones (Rayleigh scattering)
• Why not violet: sunlight contains less violet, and our eyes respond much more to blue
• Sunsets: light crosses so much air that the blue is scattered away, leaving reds and oranges
