## The peak
"The customer's message sat on Seen, the screen went dark and it was gone. Then it came back, got answered and booked."
Act 3. It gets the most scroll room (about 3.4 viewport heights on desktop), the only pinned stage, and the most
build effort.

## Tell-someone sentence
It's the site where you watch a customer's message go unanswered and vanish on a phone, then come back and get booked,
and at the end the same phone writes your own message while you fill in the form.

## Authored silence
Inside the peak, at about 0.40 to 0.48 of its progress, the phone screen is dark with one line, "No reply. Gone." This is
meant. It is not dead scroll.

## Grammar: Thread (new grammar)
One real object, the red-case phone, is the page's thread: a photographed object in the hero's world, the one
interactive surface at the peak, and the place the visitor's own enquiry begins at the close. Everything else is an
open editorial page.
- **Section unit:** a spread on ivory paper or a charcoal proof band. Grounds change on hard edges. No drift.
- **Navigation:** the floating white bar (logo, anchor links, one CTA, language switch) because visitors jump (to
  results, to the form). Phones: full-screen menu and the sticky bottom CTA.
- **Hero:** an establishing shot of the thread object in its world: layered natural-flow parallax, no pin.
- **Ending:** the thread hands over. The form sits beside the phone; the phone writes the visitor's message live.
- **Bans:** scrub video; full-bleed photography after the hero; more than one pinned act; drift; magnet; spotlight;
  section counters; an eyebrow on more than one in three sections; gradient text; full red section cards; identical
  icon-card grids; a second recurring object; any state of the phone that is not tied to the words beside it.

**Why the eight defined grammars lost.** Filmic one-shot: its devices (scrub, spotlight, magnet, one continuous film)
are heavy for phone ad traffic and it hides the anchor jumps visitors need. Chaptered editorial: forbids media above
the fold, but the plan requires the hero's largest element to be an image. Live surface: a service, not software, and
a cold owner needs a plain claim first. Continuous world: no real geography; the most fragile build on phones. Typographic
poster: the brand's proof is pictures and screenshots, not a sentence alone. Gallery: the visitor's question is "should
I believe you", not "what are the options". Split stage: the argument has two sides, but a whole page held 50/50 does not
fit a 390 px phone or the form; the two sides live inside the peak instead. Rhythmic cutlist: the voice is calm, not a
pulse.

## Signature move
**One enquiry, two endings, under your scroll.** A scroll-scrubbed chat thread on the red phone: scrolling is time.
The message arrives, sits on "Seen", the status-bar clock runs on, the screen dims and the message drops away
("No reply. Gone."). Then the thread rewinds and plays the other ending: answered, a slot chosen, booked, a reminder.
The chain Ad, Enquiry, Follow-up, Sale lights step by step beside it. Scrolling back plays it backwards. Coded in page
code; no kit device.
