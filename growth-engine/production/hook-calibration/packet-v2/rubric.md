# Hook grading rubric (copied from the readymerce-hook-lab skill, v1.1; text and spoken channels only)

Hooks are graded for how well they stop the right buyer and make that buyer need the next line. Grades are holistic,
not a count of ticks: some principles matter more for some topics. Grade honestly and never inflate.

## Grades
| Grade | Score | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| A+ | 10 | Every principle that applies is firing, with no real tradeoff |
| A | 9 | Nearly all strong; a minor tradeoff that doesn't hurt performance |
| A- | 8 | Strong, with one weakness that could be fixed: rewrite it |
| B+ | 7 | Would perform, with 1 to 2 clear gaps. Hooks at this level flop |
| B, B- | 6, 5 | Functional, or the right idea with flawed execution |
| C | 4 | Weak: several core principles broken |
| D | 2 | Broken: no context, no clarity or no promise |
| F | 0 | Confusing, misleading or off-target |

## Gates (a fail on a gate that applies caps the grade)
1. Buyer lock: the right person (the TARGET BUYER of that stack) thinks "that's me" within 2 seconds.
2. One claim: text and spoken point at the same problem or moment.
3. Rapid context: the problem is named in the first spoken sentence.
4. Sound-off: the text alone says who it's for and what the problem is.
5. Seam: the hook leads into a next line.
6. Open question at 10 s: the viewer still needs an answer.
7. No product, brand, logo, ingredient, mechanism name, price or offer in the first 3 s.
8. Facts: every number and claim is the brand's own.
9. Meta check: no "your [condition]" and no age label in the text; the person is annoyed or comic, never ashamed.

## Principles, per channel
| Principle | Spoken | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Rapid context | The problem, its cause or the failed fix in the first 6 words | The problem or moment in the first words |
| 2. Clarity | One meaning; no "me" or "it" without a name | One meaning, nothing to decode |
| 3. Contrast and curiosity | A twist: the gap between what they believe and what's true | A reason or a surprise promised |
| 4. Distillation | Sentence 1 is 8 to 14 words | 4 to 10 words |
| 5. Specificity | A number, a time, a countable person | A number, a moment, their word |
| 6. Absorption | Plain words at speaking speed | Readable in under 1.5 s |
| 7. Instant value promise | The reason or the fix, never the product | The why or the how |
| 8. Credibility anchor (bonus; don't penalise a hook that skips it) | A real fact, number or named source in lines 2 to 3 | A real number |
| 9. Buyer qualification | Their situation in their words | Their moment |
| 10. Their words | The VOC word for the problem | The VOC word |
| 11. Workaround, moment or vivid sign | The workaround, the moment, the failed fix, or one vivid sign in their exact words; never a generic symptom label or a list | Same as spoken |

## Anti-patterns (flag every one you see)
Vague superlatives; the topic arriving late; two possible readings; throat-clearing ("so basically", "I want to talk
about"); several disconnected points; jargon on a cold brain; generic fear lines that fit any topic; em dashes; bait
words (WAIT FOR IT, SHOCKING, GAME CHANGER, YOU WON'T BELIEVE); "hack" or "trick"; a category or age label as the hook
("WOMEN OVER 50"); conspiracy and disgust words ("doctors won't tell you"); a story setup before the problem; a
checklist of 4 or more symptoms.

## Rules per channel
Spoken: first word at 0.0 s. The problem, its cause or the failed fix in the first 6 words. Sentence 1 is 8 to 14
words. One vivid sign in their exact words, not a list. A twist in line 1 (a speaker who shouldn't talk, mockery, a
dare, a confession, an edge word, a specific countable person; a plain conditional with an age label is not enough).
Kill the failed fix calmly, or flip the misread. The value promise is the reason or the fix, never the product. Calm
authority beats conspiracy and anger.
Text: on screen from frame 1 to about 3 s. 4 to 10 words. It states the claim, the character's line or the hidden
cause. Never a category label, an age label, "hack" or "trick", an offer, the brand, a founder line, or "your
[condition]". Use "she", "I", the situation, or "why" plus the problem.

## Hook types (the menu)
Character in place; Villain mockery; Stop doing X; Hidden cause ("This is why..."); Fear moment; What would happen if;
Named person plus habit (specific and countable); Confession (first person, a number, an edge); Workaround reveal;
Witness or overheard line; Impossible scale or absurd literal cause; Two futures; Misdirection; Challenge.
